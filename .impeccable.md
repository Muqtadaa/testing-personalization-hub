# Impeccable — Project Design Context

## Design Context

### Users
Internal **Testing & Personalization Team** (the CRO function within the Growth organization) and the **business stakeholders** they serve across the Online Store, Marketplace, and Rewards App lines of business at the fictional retailer Sample Retail Co. Two distinct audiences:
- **Practitioners** (the T&P team): experienced with experimentation, FX, RICE, Jira. Want speed and precision.
- **Stakeholders/requesters** (marketers, PMs, ops): often *not* fluent in testing terminology. Use the Intake & Brief Builder to turn a rough idea into a structured brief that gets filed to Jira.

Context of use: focused work sessions on desktop. The job to be done is moving ideas from **intake → structured brief → evidence**: submitting/qualifying test ideas, browsing the FX process and backlog, and reading out results.

### Brand Personality
Three words: **confident, clear, businesslike.** Professional and trustworthy — this is filed into Jira and read by serious stakeholders. Helpful and plain-spoken, never cute. **No emojis, emoticons, or decorative symbols** anywhere in product copy or generated content (a hard rule already enforced in the intake/brief prompts). Voice: a thoughtful program manager guiding you, not a passive form.

### Aesthetic Direction
Clean, modern, high-contrast and content-first. The signature look is a **dark charcoal (#38353F) hero/nav with a single lime (#B1FF33) accent** over an otherwise light, white working surface. Lime carries "positive/active"; a tuned `critical` red is the only other semantic color. Typeface is **Inter**. Generous spacing, card-based layouts, subtle motion (`animate-fade-up`, typing dots) that respects `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- **Theme:** light working surfaces with deliberate dark accent sections (hero, nav, footer). Not a full dark-mode app.
- **Anti-references:** nothing playful, gimmicky, or consumer-flashy; no emoji/decoration; no internal jargon or doc-pointer language in user-facing copy.

### Design Principles
1. **Clarity over cleverness.** Plain, specific language. Say what something is and what to do next. Never expose internal jargon, file names, or "source of truth" doc references in the UI.
2. **Meet the least-expert user.** Copy and flows must work for a stakeholder who doesn't know testing terminology; lead with their words, not ours (e.g. "Context", "Desired outcome", not "RICE confidence").
3. **Tokens, not hex.** Always use the semantic Tailwind tokens (`text-body`/`muted`/`subtle`, `on-dark*`, `lime`, `charcoal`, `critical*`) — they're WCAG-tuned (AA/AAA). Never hardcode colors or inline tracking.
4. **One accent, high contrast.** Lime is the single accent; keep it purposeful. Maintain strong contrast and AA-min on every text/background pairing.
5. **Quiet, professional polish.** Motion and flourish are subtle and functional; respect reduced motion. The interface should feel calm and credible, never loud.
